INSTRUCTOR TRAINING MANUAL — KAIZEN AI INTEGRATION METHOD

Version 1.1 — Canonical, Workbook‑Aligned

This document is the authoritative Instructor Training Manual for teaching the **Kaizen AI Integration Method**. It is fully aligned with the Student Workbook, which is the source of truth. Where conflicts could exist, the Student Workbook always prevails.

This manual explains **how to teach** the workflow. It does not redefine the workflow.

---

SECTION 1 — PURPOSE OF THIS INSTRUCTOR MANUAL

This Instructor Training Manual exists to teach instructors how to teach others the **Kaizen AI Integration Method**.

It does **not** teach the workflow itself — that is the role of the Student Workbook.

This manual instead explains:

* what instructors must emphasize
* what rules cannot be broken
* how to guide students step‑by‑step
* how to verify student understanding
* how to correct common student mistakes
* how to enforce strict workflow discipline

This manual is designed to be used **together with** the Student Workbook.

---

SECTION 2 — INSTRUCTOR RESPONSIBILITIES

Instructors are responsible for:

* enforcing workflow discipline at all times
* ensuring students follow every step in order
* correcting students immediately when rules are broken
* preventing drift, shortcuts, or improvisation
* modeling correct Kaizen behavior in real time
* confirming comprehension before advancing
* ensuring the Holy Tenet is never violated

This is a **coaching role**, not a lecturing role.

The goal is to guide students into using **AI** correctly, deliberately, and reliably in real work.

---

SECTION 3 — INSTRUCTOR REQUIRED PREPARATION

Before training anyone, instructors must:

* read this Instructor Manual in full
* read the Student Workbook in full
* understand every step of the Kaizen AI Integration workflow
* understand why each step exists
* understand common failure modes
* perform at least one complete demonstration project themselves
* practice adversarial testing using free Grok, free Gemini, and free Qwen
* be able to explain each step in simple, student‑friendly language

An instructor who cannot execute the workflow end‑to‑end must not teach it.

SECTION 4 — INSTRUCTOR’S GUIDE TO THE KAIZEN AI INTEGRATION WORKFLOW

This section mirrors the Student Workbook exactly. Instructors must not add, remove, reorder, or rename steps.

### 4.1 Teach Step 1 — Define the Scope

Students must clearly define:

* what they are attempting to create
* for whom
* the required format (new or existing)
* what must be included
* what must be excluded

Instructor emphasis:

* vague scope creates unstable output
* format persistence must be explicit
* unclear objectives guarantee drift

---

### 4.2 Teach Step 2 — Break the Work Into Chunks

Students must:

* divide work into narrow, self‑contained chunks
* state exactly what each chunk covers
* instruct the AI to focus on one chunk only

Instructor emphasis:

* one chunk at a time is non‑negotiable
* multi‑chunk generation recreates overload
* chunking reduces hallucination risk

---

### 4.3 Teach Step 3 — Verify Output and Prevent Drift

Students must:

* read or listen to every AI response in full
* verify correctness before saving
* correct errors immediately

Instructor emphasis:

* AI output is draft material until verified
* confidence does not equal correctness
* long threads may require restarting a new chat

---

### 4.4 Teach Step 4 — Chunk, Check, Save, Repeat

Students must:

* complete one chunk
* review it carefully
* explicitly save or lock it
* only then move to the next chunk

Instructor emphasis:

* repetition is intentional
* consistency matters more than speed
* skipping review compounds errors

---

### 4.5 Teach Step 5 (Optional) — Stress Test Using External Models

This step is optional.

If chosen, students must:

* submit finalized work to an external model (free Grok, free Gemini, or free Qwen)
* request critique or attack
* treat output as adversarial diagnostics, not authority

Instructor emphasis:

* disagreement reveals weak points
* external models lack full context
* stress testing is a diagnostic tool

---

### 4.6 Teach Step 6 (Optional) — Return External Critiques to the Primary AI

If stress testing is performed, this step must be executed correctly.

Students must:

* copy critiques word‑for‑word
* return them without framing, filtering, or paraphrasing

Instructor emphasis:

* exact wording preserves signal
* AI evaluates critiques in context
* the human decides what changes

SECTION 5 — TEACHING STRATEGY

### 5.1 Keep Students Focused

Students will attempt to:

* jump ahead
* skip steps
* multitask
* skim AI output
* paraphrase critiques

The instructor must stop these behaviors immediately.

---

### 5.2 Maintain a Slow, Steady Rhythm

Training must not feel rushed.

Instructors should:

* demonstrate each step clearly
* allow questions
* confirm comprehension before advancing

---

### 5.3 Use Controlled Examples

Start with:

* tiny examples
* single paragraphs
* one‑chunk exercises

Do not begin with full documents.

---

### 5.4 Correct Mistakes in Real Time

When a student:

* breaks the chunking rule
* edits locked content
* skips verification
* violates the Holy Tenet

The instructor must immediately explain why the behavior is incorrect.

---

### 5.5 Encourage Multi‑Model Testing

Teach students to:

* expect flawed critiques
* use disagreement as signal
* return critiques exactly as written

---

SECTION 6 — COMMON STUDENT MISTAKES AND CORRECTIONS

### 6.1 Rushing

Correction:

* slow the student down
* reinforce structure over speed

---

### 6.2 Breaking the Chunk Rule

Correction:

* stop the work
* restart the chunk
* explain hallucination risk

---

### 6.3 Editing Locked Content

Correction:

* deny the edit
* explain stability through locking

---

### 6.4 Not Reading AI Output Fully

Correction:

* require full review
* remind them of the Holy Tenet

---

### 6.5 Improvising Steps

Correction:

* enforce the defined sequence
* prevent deviation

---

### 6.6 Misusing Adversarial Testing

Correction:

* clarify optional status
* enforce correct execution if chosen

---

### 6.7 Ignoring Confusion

Correction:

* stop progress
* clarify immediately
* resume only after understanding

---

SECTION 7 — THE TRAINER’S HARD RULES

1. The Holy Tenet — Students must read or actively listen to every AI response.
2. No Skipping Steps — Order is mandatory.
3. No Editing Locked Sections — Locked means locked.
4. All Feedback Must Be Examined — No skimming critiques.
5. Enforce Discipline — Stop and correct deviations immediately.
6. No Guessing — Students must not invent steps.
7. No Ignoring Confusion — Confusion requires immediate clarification.
