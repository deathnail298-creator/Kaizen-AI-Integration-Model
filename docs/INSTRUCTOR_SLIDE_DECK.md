📘 SECTION 3 — CHUNKING
Slide 7 — Why Chunking Is Mandatory

LLMs drift with long tasks

Humans over-explain

Chunking prevents instruction overload

Slide 8 — Chunking Rules

One logical action per chunk

No dependencies inside a chunk

Only output what is asked

Hard stop: don’t move to next chunk early

Slide 9 — Hands-On Drill 1

Follow the workbook instructions exactly.

Exercise: Rewrite a 2-page assignment into 8–12 chunks following rules.

📘 SECTION 4 — INSTRUCTION LOCKING
Slide 10 — What Locking Means

Pinning rules into the AI’s working memory

Reinforcement through repetition

Creates stability across long tasks

Slide 11 — Locking Template

“Repeat the instructions back to me.”

“Confirm my rules step-by-step.”

“Do not begin until I say ‘Begin’.”

Slide 12 — Hands-On Drill 2

Follow the workbook instructions exactly.

Instructor gives unstable instructions → students stabilize them with locking.

No single model can be authority

Disagreement is a signal, not a failure
